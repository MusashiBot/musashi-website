## The Big Number: October Was Prediction Markets' Biggest Month Ever

Per a16z Frontier ([Substack note](https://substack.com/@a16zfrontier/note/c-383208197), Nov 9), October 2026 set all-time highs on almost every measure, each up from September's records:

- **Contracts traded (notional): $123.6B** (up 58%), 1.5x all of 2024 and 2025 combined ($82B)
- **Cash paid in: $22.7B** (up 25%), counting parlays at the cents paid, not the $1 payout. That alone beats all of 2025 ($20.9B)
- **Trades: 644M** (up 36%)
- **Fees: $647M** (up 27%)
- **Open interest: $3.1B** (up 42%)

Why builders care: $647M in monthly fees is the revenue pool tooling, data and distribution startups are fighting over, and the gap between notional ($123.6B) and cash paid in ($22.7B) is a reminder to pick your metric carefully in a pitch deck.

"Before midnight EST" last traded at 64¢ and "before noon Nov 14" at 94¢ ([Kalshi](https://kalshi.com/markets/kxfaagroundstoplift)). ## Build This

1. **Incident-market resolution toolkit.** Cause markets need evidence standards, such as an official finding vs. a vendor statement, plus clear rules for overlapping outcomes and deadlines. Kalshi's ground-stop cause market has a contractor outcome at 90¢ while "equipment or system failure" sits at 74¢. *Why now:* venues are listing these within 40 minutes, before anyone has written a playbook.
2. **Government-ops event oracle.** Turn FAA command-center advisories, DOT statements and agency status boards into machine-readable listing triggers and settlement sources. *Why now:* Kalshi's lift contracts resolve on an "official announcement," and the FAA's advisory windows kept moving (11 PM, then 11:30 PM ET) ([Edit USA](https://www.editusa.com/news/faa-nationwide-ground-stop-november-13-2026), [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/nation/2026/11/13/faa-orders-nationwide-groundstop-at-us-airports-citing-safety/92765499007/)).
3. **Restricted-trader screening API.** Employer and clearance screening, plus contract-level restrictions, sold to DCMs, brokers and front-ends. *Why now:* the Oversight memo floats either a full ban or limits on government-related contracts. Platforms gave five briefings on detection, and the Senate already bars members and staff ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/prediction-markets-comer-government-trading-ban/)).
4. **Nex readiness harness for market makers.** End-to-end latency and fill-quality benchmarks comparing the Nex sandbox with the current CLOB. *Why now:* Polymarket's published gains are "internal exchange timings, not e2e." Quoting logic tuned for 1.5-second acks will need retuning ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/polymarket-nex-sandbox-trading-capacity/)).
5. **White-label play-money forecasting.** Points-based markets for communities, creators and internal enterprise forecasting. *Why now:* Meta is dogfooding a "cred" model while real-money venues fight state suits. In Europe, Lyd Markets is taking the gambling-license route instead ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2026-11-13-meta-tests-prediction-market-app-using-cred-points-instead-cash-wsj-440215), [Øresund Startup News](https://oresundstartups.com/lyd-markets-wants-to-launch-a-regulated-european-challenger-to-polymarket/)).

## Ecosystem & Builder Resources

- **Polymarket Nex sandbox.** This is a Rust rewrite of the DeFi CLOB. It runs 100K commands/sec sustained (150K burst), vs. 10K today. In internal timings, p99 order placement fell from 1.55s to 3.76ms. There are no breaking changes to requests or responses. Polymarket plans four weeks of shadow testing and a conditional mid-December launch, and WebSocket upgrades may ship sooner ([Josh Stevens on X](https://x.com/devjoshstevens/status/2121241806469796103), [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/polymarket-nex-sandbox-trading-capacity/)). Chinese coverage flagged the caveat: "该指标并非端到端延迟," meaning the metric isn't end-to-end latency ([Mitrade/金色财经](https://www.mitrade.com/zh/insights/news/live-news/article-3-2254117-20261114)).
- **CFTC sues Arkansas** to block the state's enforcement against Kalshi ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9325-26)). At Cantor's Convergence conference, Chair Selig said: "Somebody says it's gambling, somebody says it's a derivative. That's not something that should be at the risk of going to jail" ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-11-13-cftcs-selig-accuses-states-trying-lock-up-prediction-market-founders-440199)).
